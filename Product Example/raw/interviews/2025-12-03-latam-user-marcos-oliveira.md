# User Interview — Marcos Oliveira, IT Manager, LogisBR
**Date:** 2025-12-03
**Interviewer:** Priya Nair (Senior PM, Acme Support Products)
**Duration:** 35 minutes
**Format:** Video call (Google Meet) — conducted partially in English, partially with Priya's help translating key points
**Context:** LogisBR is a mid-market logistics company in São Paulo, Brazil. ~60 Acme seats, primarily for ops and route planning teams. They onboarded to Acme in January 2025. LogisBR is part of a cohort of LATAM clients Acme has been onboarding as part of its regional expansion pilot.

---

## Pre-interview notes

LATAM expansion is a key initiative for Acme in 2026. LogisBR is one of ~12 clients from the first LATAM cohort. The CS team flagged that LogisBR has a disproportionately high ticket volume relative to their seat count — about 3x the average for a client their size. This interview is to understand the support friction. 

Marcos is not a native English speaker. His English is functional but he noted he's more comfortable in Portuguese.

---

## Transcript (condensed, paraphrased except where quoted)

**On first impression of the chatbot:**
Marcos said he was "excited" when the chatbot launched because it promised faster support. But within the first few weeks, it became clear the bot only worked in English. "We tried to write in Portuguese. The bot answers in English. My team doesn't read English well. So we have a problem."

He described how his team's approach evolved: they started typing in Portuguese, the bot responded in English, they'd paste the English into Google Translate, interpret the answer (sometimes incorrectly), try to apply it, fail, and then submit a ticket anyway. 

> "We use the chatbot like... a step we have to do before we can get real help. We know it won't work, but we do it because it's there."

**On the ticket volume:**
He confirmed his team submits a lot of tickets — he said it's "normal for us" because the bot doesn't help. He estimated 70-80% of their support needs could be handled by the bot "if it was in Portuguese." The remaining 20-30% are complex technical issues that would need a human anyway.

Specific pain: Acme recently released a new route optimization feature. The bot's documentation and troubleshooting for that feature was entirely in English. His team couldn't use it without significant delay. They ended up waiting 2 days for a Portuguese-speaking support agent to be available.

> "Two days. For something your bot could probably answer in 2 minutes if it spoke Portuguese."

**On the impact to his team:**
Three of his direct reports have mentioned the language barrier as a daily frustration. One of them has been writing support tickets entirely in Portuguese and waiting — "she says at least someone eventually responds in Portuguese, even if it takes longer."

He also mentioned that onboarding new employees to Acme is harder because the help center and chatbot are English-only. New hires who don't read English have to depend on Marcos or one other bilingual colleague to translate, which creates a bottleneck.

**On Spanish:**
When asked about Spanish (Acme also has clients in Mexico and Colombia), Marcos said he has colleagues at a sister company in Bogotá with the same problem. He doesn't know their experience with Acme specifically but said, "I'd assume it's the same."

**On alternatives:**
He mentioned that a competitor product they evaluated before choosing Acme (he didn't name it) had Portuguese support. They chose Acme for features, not support quality. He said if the language issue isn't fixed in the next "6 months or so," his leadership will revisit the tool choice at renewal.

> "The product is good. The support experience — for us — is not."

**On what he'd want:**
Very clear: "The bot in Portuguese. That's it. If the bot answers in Portuguese, most of our problems go away." He also mentioned it would help if the help center articles were translated, not just the bot — but his priority was the bot.

He'd also appreciate being able to set a language preference at the account level so his whole team benefits, rather than each user needing to configure something.

**On willingness to refer:**
He said he'd referred Acme to two other companies in the LATAM region but "told them to know that support is in English only." This is likely suppressing referrals.

---

## Interviewer observations

Marcos is patient and constructive. The frustration is clear but not hostile. The workaround pattern (bot → translate → fail → ticket) is deeply ingrained already in only 11 months. This is compounding — every new user at LogisBR inherits this broken workflow.

The renewal risk is real — he flagged it unprompted. 6 months = around June 2026.

The referral suppression angle is unexpected and worth surfacing to the sales team.
